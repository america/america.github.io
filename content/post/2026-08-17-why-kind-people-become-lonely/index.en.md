Why do the kindest people end up the loneliest? The video frames this not as one dramatic event, but as something that builds up quietly, day by day.

---

## Putting Others First, Yourself Last

It starts with thinking too much about other people's feelings, and putting your own needs last. You can't say no when someone asks for help, and before you know it, you're always the one helping — never the one being helped.

---

## Sacrifice Without Return, and a Smile That Says "I'm Fine"

From there, you start sacrificing without expecting anything back, without even noticing it's happening. Even when it hurts, you get used to smiling and saying "I'm fine."

---

## Being Alone Starts to Feel Easier

Eventually, being alone starts to feel easier. You put distance between yourself and the world, and stop opening your heart more than you have to. Rather than trust people, you start choosing not to get hurt.

---

## It's Not That You Like Being Alone

But, the video continues, that isn't because you want to be alone.

> It's not that I like being alone. I just don't want to get hurt anymore.

I just want to feel safe — I just want to trust someone — that's how the video closes.

---

## Summary

* Kind people tend to prioritize others' feelings and put their own needs last
* Unable to say no, they keep sacrificing without expecting anything back, and learn to smile through the pain
* Over time, being alone starts to feel easier, and they put distance between themselves and the world
* They start choosing not to get hurt over trusting people
* But this isn't a desire for loneliness — it's simply not wanting to get hurt anymore, while still, underneath it all, wanting to feel safe enough to trust someone

Note that this is just one perspective offered by the video, not an academic claim.
