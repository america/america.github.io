On 2 November 2015 I went to "School of Security," a lecture course run by the journalist
Hiromichi Ugaya. It was a beginners' series on national security — its motto was "no prep
required, come empty-handed."

Around then I had bought and read Ugaya's *From Hiroshima to Fukushima: A Strange Journey
Through Nuclear Power* (expanded edition, Yujin Shoin, 288 pages). It's a reportage that
treats nuclear weapons and nuclear power as twin brothers, tracing the technology from the
Los Alamos desert through Hiroshima and Nagasaki to reactors installed across Japan — an
ensemble piece that also follows lives like Robert Oppenheimer's and Enrico Fermi's. The
expanded edition adds a chapter, "From Oppenheimer's Tragedy to the ALPS Ocean Release."

At the izakaya after the lecture, I remember talking about the book. Two things stuck:

- That people abroad know more about Fukushima than people in Japan do.
- That the photographs were beautiful.
